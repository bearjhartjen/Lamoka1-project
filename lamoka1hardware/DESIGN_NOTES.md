The physical hardware design of the Lamoka1:


The Lamoka1 is a charger (takes a wide range of input sources, specifically solar, but any source with the required voltage is good) and a inverter (takes the battery power and turns it into AC, that AC can be turned into 120 Vrms for use with North American products. Or many many waveforms and systems, configurable by programming the RP2350 to do things with the H bridge) 

The inversion part of the unit is relatively simple, the unit takes anywhere from 5-40v (max input current is 30a) then steps that up to 48v, that 48v gets put into a frequency generator which goes to a transformer and up to high voltage, this pulsed output then gets smoothed out to 180v DC, this DC then goes into an H bridge, articulated by a RP2350. The output of the H bridge goes into the wave reform section which can ingadge the LC filter for making near perfect sine waves, (I didnt want the H bridge to be soley responsible for generating a pure sine wave as the switching frequency would be too high to make accurate sine waves).

There will have to be many sensors on this unit for, Temperature, Voltage feedback (waveforms, Vrms calculation) 

The whole unit will be locked into a constant current situation. No more than 30 amps shall pass through any component. The output power of the inverter will be directly controlled by the input voltage to the boost converter. A maximum of 30a will be allowed through any power section, If the input to the boost converter is say 7v, the inverter, if a high draw device is plugged in, will demand that power, in our example lets model a 7v input with a power demand on the inverter of 500w (we will assume this item to be a resistive load for simpliciy, the resistor is valued at 28.8ohms) the inverter will simply demand the boost converter for 500w (we arent thinking about inefficiency here, in a real situation there would be more than 500w) now the boost converter needs 500w, but the input for it is only 7v, that means you need to go from 7v to 48v (48v is the output of the boost converter) and you need to also supply 500w, well if we divide 500w by our input of 7v we get a current demand from our source supply of roughly 71.4a, that is a signifigant amount of power, that means that our inductors will have to handle 71.4a, if we divided the load into the two interleaved which is how this unit is setup, each inductor has to increase the voltage from 7v to 48v and do that at around 30a each, first off, the inductors picked are not made for that kind of power, but neither is anything else in the boost converter. Thus we need a constant current, this means that no more than 30a can ever enter the through the door of the boost converter, keeping all of the beautiful components safe. Instead of measuring current at the source (front of boost converter) we might as well just restrict the output of the inverter according to the input voltage as we know that the inverters output is what would be demanding the power anyway, and we need a crap ton of sensing and data around that inverter anyways for smart features, near perfect sine waves, power factor correcting, etc. 

How does the Lamoka1 protect an absolute disaster with constant cuurent? Well, it depends entirely weather or not you are using the dumb resistive mode, or normal operation mode, if you are in dumb mode, the unit will automatically adjust the Vrms output of the inverter down, as per ohms law, the lower voltage at a constant resistance, the amperage will drop, this saves our device as it keeps an output going while making sure everything else is running well. If you are in normal mode the unit will simply stop outputting anything from the inverter, this is acheived by just stopping the switching on the H bridge then no current will flow through and out. There will be some lighting features that signal things like this.

The LM5122 (the controller IC for the boost converter) has a MODE pin, to put it simply, we can choose wether or not the boost converter is working in Forced PWM mode (normal mode) or Didoe emulaiton mode (sleep mode). The normal mode makes the boost converter work hard to generate a 250khz signal for the two inductors, this means that in normal mode if no load is being drawn the boost converter is just charging and discharging all the MOSFETs gates, and other dumb things that dont need to happen if there isnt even a load. The sleep mode makes the boost converter only work when it needs to, we have a big capacitor bank on the end of the boost converter, the IC takes this to our advantage, in sleep mode the unit is only looking to keep around 48v, as soon as the FB and COMP pin of the LM5122 sense there is a voltage droop from 48v the unit gets turned back on, if it is figured that 48v is there then the MOSFETs just dont get any signal so the inductore is isolated. (i need to figure out exactly how the COMP and FB pins do this and what the threshold is for turning back on) Overall sleep mode is very helpful for very low outputs from the inverter, or just when the unit is idling (i want it setup so that when a certain amount of time goes by with little to no power draw the unit will enter sleep mode then just snap back on when needed).
