1.1  Design Overview
AI-Assisted —
The board is a single-sheet microcontroller development platform built around U1, a Raspberry Pi RP2040 dual-core Arm Cortex-M0+ microcontroller (marked SC1511-A4) in a QFN-60 package with an exposed pad. The design pairs the microcontroller with a USB 2.0 Type-C interface, a 12.000 MHz crystal timebase, a ten-node addressable RGB LED chain, a general-purpose I/O header, and a single momentary pushbutton. The board is fully instrumented with flying-probe test points across its signal and power nets.

Processing and Clocking

U1 provides the digital core, on-chip QSPI flash interface, ADC inputs, and USB full-speed PHY. The system timebase is X1, an Abracon ABM8-272-T3 12.000 MHz fundamental-mode crystal with a 10 pF load capacitance, matched to two 15p loading capacitors and a series/return network on the XIN/XOUT terminals. The crystal is rated -40°C to +85°C. Programming and debug are served by the RP2040 SWD port.

Interfaces

External connectivity is provided by J3, a 14-pin USB Type-C receptacle wired for USB 2.0 signalling, with the two CC pins terminated through 5.1k configuration resistors R2 and R3 establishing the board as an upstream-facing (device) port. The D+/D- pair reaches U1 through 27 series resistors R5 and R6. J1 is a 1x13 vertical 2.54 mm pin header breaking out thirteen GPIO lines and supply/ground for expansion. SW1 is a momentary pushbutton on GPIO0. Ten Worldsemi WS2812B integrated RGB LEDs (D1-D10) form a single-wire NZR data chain running from U1 through a level-translating buffer; the WS2812B operates over -25°C to +80°C junction. U3, a Texas Instruments SN74AHCT1G125 single tri-state buffer, level-shifts the 3V3 LED data output up to the 5V logic level required by the WS2812B chain.

Power Tree

Board power originates from the USB Type-C VBUS at 5 V, forming the 5V rail that supplies the LED chain and the buffer. U2, an Advanced Monolithic Systems AMS1117-3.3 SOT-223 linear regulator, derives the 3V3 rail from 5V; its datasheet typical output is 3.300 V. The RP2040 on-chip core regulator generates the 1V1 core rail through switching node VREG_LX and the 3.3u inductor L1, and VREG_AVDD supplies the regulator's analog domain. Detailed rail sourcing and pin connectivity are covered in the power analysis section.

Temperature Range

The assembled operating range is bounded by the WS2812B LEDs at -25°C to +80°C; the crystal (-40°C to +85°C), regulator, and buffer are all rated at or beyond that window.

Boundary Scan

No boundary-scan-capable device with a matched BSDL model is present on this board; the RP2040 exposes an SWD debug port rather than an IEEE 1149.1 TAP, and no other device provides a boundary-scan chain. Interconnect and structural coverage rely on the flying-probe test-point instrumentation, which is addressed in the Design-for-Test section.

4.2.1  Power Tree Overview
AI-Assisted —
Board power originates off-board at 5 V: USB Type-C VBUS (J3 pins A4/A9/B4/B9) and header J1 pin 13 form the 5V rail. The rail is bulk-decoupled with 111 µF total (1x100u + 1x10u + 10x100n). 5V feeds three loads: the AMS1117-3.3 LDO (U2) input, the SN74AHCT1G125 buffer (U3) VCC, and the VDD of the ten WS2812B pixels (D1-D10). U2 produces the 3V3 rail (VOUT typ 3.300 V, AMS1117 datasheet p.2), which supplies the RP2354 (U1) IOVDD/ADC_AVDD/USB_OTP_VDD group and the internal-regulator input VREG_VIN (pin 49). U1's on-chip buck regulator switches on VREG_LX (pin 48) through the 3.3u inductor L1 to generate the 1V1 core rail that returns to VREG_FB (pin 50) and powers the DVDD pins. Sequencing is monotonic and self-timed: 5V rises first, the LDO brings up 3V3, then the RP2354 internal regulator brings up 1V1; RUN (pin 26) is released by the 10k pull-up R8 to 3V3, so the core comes out of reset only once 3V3 is valid. All supply and ground pins of both power devices (U2 VIN/VOUT/GND; U1 VREG_VIN/VREG_PGND/VREG_LX/VREG_FB) sit on nets with proper DC paths.
4.2.2  AMS1117-3.3 Linear Regulator (U2)
AI-Assisted —
The fixed 3.3 V SOT-223 LDO takes 5V on VIN (pin 3) and drives 3V3 on the doubled VOUT pins (2, 4); GND (pin 1) returns to the ground rail. Input decoupling on 5V is generous. The dropout at 0.8 A is 1.1 V typ (datasheet p.3), so 1.7 V of headroom from 5 V is ample. At an estimated few-hundred-mA core/IO load the SOT-223 dissipates roughly 0.5 W (1.7 V x 0.3 A); with θ_JA 55-80 °C/W (datasheet Table 1, p.5) the junction rise stays well below the 165 °C thermal-shutdown point. The concern is output capacitance: the AMS1117 datasheet (p.4) calls for a 22 µF solid tantalum output capacitor for loop compensation. The 3V3 node carries C13 10u + C14 4.7u + C17 100n + C18 100n (about 14.9 µF nominal, all ceramic, further derated under DC bias) plus C16 4.7u behind R4 33. The total is below the 22 µF minimum and uses low-ESR ceramic rather than the specified tantalum, which can compromise the regulator's stability margin.
4.2.3  RP2354 On-Chip Core Regulator (U1, 1V1)
AI-Assisted —
The RP2354's integrated buck regulator generates the 1.1 V core. VREG_VIN (pin 49) is powered from 3V3; the switching node VREG_LX (pin 48) drives inductor L1 (3.3u) to the 1V1 output; VREG_FB (pin 50) senses 1V1; VREG_PGND (pin 47) returns to GND. The 1V1 rail carries C15 4.7u + C19 100n (4.8 µF) of output decoupling to GND, and the DVDD core pins (6, 23, 39) sit on it. The regulator's analog supply VREG_AVDD (pin 46) is filtered from 3V3 by R4 33 with C16 4.7u to GND, forming an RC supply filter. The output-referenced feedback and inductor topology match an internally compensated buck; L1 is a 3.3u part, consistent with the switching converter. No external ground- or supply-pin faults exist on the regulator pins.
4.2.4  5 V Input, USB and LED Signal Domain
AI-Assisted —
USB Type-C is configured as a sink/UFP: CC1 and CC2 carry 5.1k pull-downs to GND (R2 on Net-(J3-CC1), R3 on USB_CC2), the correct Rd terminations. D+/D- pairs are strapped together and series-terminated with 27 R (R6 on USB_CONN_DP, R5 on USB_CONN_DM) into U1's USB_DP/USB_DM. The connector shield bonds to logic ground through net-tie NT2. The WS2812B pixels run their VDD on 5V, inside the device's 4.5-5.5 V operating window (WS2812B datasheet p.3); each pixel has a 100n local bypass (C1-C10), meeting the datasheet's 0.1 µF-per-device recommendation (p.5). Because the WS2812B input threshold is VIH = 0.7·VDD = 3.5 V at 5 V VDD, the 3.3 V GPIO11 cannot drive DIN directly; U3 (SN74AHCT1G125, TTL-compatible, VIH min 2 V) correctly translates the level to 5 V, with its OE (pin 1) tied low to keep the output enabled, and R1 330 R provides series damping into the D1 DIN. The bootsel/QSPI_SS sense (R7 1k from BTN to QSPI_SS_N, R9 10k pull-up, SW1 to GND) is the standard button arrangement; the RP2354A's on-die flash makes external QSPI memory unnecessary, so the unused QSPI pins are intentionally left open.
4.3  Observations
AI-Assisted —
The 5V input has no series overcurrent fuse or TVS between the Type-C connector and the downstream loads; for a development board this is acceptable but leaves VBUS unprotected against a downstream short or transient. The 12.000 MHz crystal X1 (Abracon ABM8-272, CL 10 pF) uses two 15p load capacitors (C20, C21) with a 1k series resistor R11 on the XOUT leg; the resulting effective load of about 11.5 pF (7.5 pF from the two caps plus stray) slightly exceeds the crystal's 10 pF specification and will pull the frequency marginally low, still within the USB clock budget. Every regulator supply and ground pin has been traced to a valid DC node. All capacitors on the board are ceramic (unpolarized), so no polarity risk exists.

11.1.1  Microcontroller U1 (SC1511-A4 / RP2350)
AI-Assisted —
The core switch-mode supply matches the RP2350 reference design: VREG_LX drives a 3.3µF inductor (L1, AOTA-B201610S3R3, 3.3u) into the 1V1 core rail, VREG_FB senses 1V1 for regulation, and the core rail carries 4.8µF of decoupling (1x4.7u + 1x100n). VREG_VIN and the analog rails derive from 3V3; VREG_AVDD is correctly filtered by R4 (33) with C16 (4.7u) to the pin, and ADC_AVDD ties to 3V3. Both ground returns, U1_61 GND and U1_47 VREG_PGND, sit on the GND net with a valid DC path. RUN is held by R8 (10k) to 3V3 as reset pull-up. The BOOTSEL path is correct: SW1 pulls the QSPI chip-select node low through R7 (1k) against R9 (10k) pull-up on GPIO0/BTN. The external QSPI data/clock pins are intentionally left unconnected, consistent with use of the device's internal 2MB flash. USB D+/D- use 27R series resistors R6/R5 to the Type-C receptacle, and CC1/CC2 each carry an independent 5.1k Rd pull-down (R2, R3) — the correct sink (UFP) termination. One observation: the 3V3 rail feeds six IOVDD pins plus ADC_AVDD, VREG_VIN, USB_OTP_VDD and QSPI_IOVDD, but only two 100n high-frequency capacitors (plus 10u and 4.7u bulk) are present; Raspberry Pi guidance suggests ~100nF per supply pin, so additional local decoupling would improve margin. Not a functional defect on a development board.
11.1.2  Crystal X1 (ABM8-272-T3, 12 MHz)
AI-Assisted —
X1 provides the 12.000 MHz reference. Terminal 1 connects directly to XIN and terminal 2 returns through R11 (1k) to XOUT — the series drive resistor recommended for the RP2350 crystal oscillator, which limits drive level against the crystal's 200µW maximum. Load capacitors C20 and C21 (15p each, C0G) sit from each crystal terminal to GND; for the datasheet load capacitance of 10 pF the required per-leg value with a few pF of stray is ~14–16 pF, so 15p is well matched. The two case pads (X1_2, X1_4) are grounded. Frequency tolerance and stability of ±30 ppm are comfortably within USB full-speed timing requirements. Wiring and passive values are correct per the Abracon ABM8-272 datasheet.
11.1.3  Level Buffer U3 (SN74AHCT1G125DBVR)
AI-Assisted —
U3 is used as a 3.3 V→5 V level translator for the WS2812B data line, exploiting the TTL-compatible AHCT inputs: input A (pin 2) receives the 3.3 V LED_DATA_3V3 signal from U1 GPIO11, and the buffered output Y (pin 4) drives the LED chain through a 330 series resistor (R1). Per the TI SN74AHCT1G125 datasheet VIH(min) is 2 V, so the 3.3 V logic high is reliably recognized. VCC (pin 5) is on the 5V rail, within the recommended 4.5–5.5 V range, and GND (pin 3) is on GND. Output-enable OE (pin 1) is tied to GND, holding the output permanently enabled — a legitimate fixed-enable configuration. For bench debug a resistor soft-tie plus a test point on OE would improve testability, but this is a preference, not a fault. The 5V rail carries ample 100n decoupling adjacent to the device, satisfying the datasheet's 0.1 µF bypass recommendation. The fitted DBVR (SOT-23-5) order option is production status; only the DBVT/DCKT reel variants are obsolete, so no lifecycle concern applies here.
11.1.4  Ground and Shield Net Ties (NT1, NT2)
AI-Assisted —
NT1 is a deliberate copper net tie merging the regulator ground reference into the main GND node as a single star point; both pins reside on GND. This is an intentional join, not a shorted or duplicated net. NT2 implements the Type-C shield-to-logic-ground bond, connecting the receptacle SHIELD/CHASSIS_GND to GND. Both are intended connectivity. The direct shield bond is acceptable; a parallel resistor-plus-capacitor bond is an alternative EMC option but not required, and either choice is a design preference.
